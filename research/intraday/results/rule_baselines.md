# Rule baselines (family `rule_baselines`, kind `baseline`)

Script: `experiments/intraday/rule_baselines.py`. Long/flat spot, BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT, walk-forward
OOS window [2020-01-01, 2026-07-01), every config logged at 5 and 10 bps/side, seed 0 (the rules are
deterministic). Bars: 5m cache resampled with `incomplete="drop"` (drops 12 / 28 / 48 incomplete
15m / 1h / 4h bars over 2017-09..2026-06; rolling windows count bars, so a window spanning a dropped
bar reaches one bar further back in time).

## Pre-registration (written 2026-11-05 before any OOS run)

24 configs per symbol, 48 runs in total (x2 costs). No config will be added after seeing OOS
results unless it is logged and marked `post_hoc` below.

Windows are in bars of the timeframe (1h: 24 = 1 day, 168 = 1 week; 4h: 6 = 1 day, 42 = 1 week,
180 = 30 days; 15m: 96 = 1 day).

2017-09.. history so the windows are warm at 2020-01-01; the target is then sliced to the OOS window

of `splits.walk_forward_splits` (purged by `t1`), never the test month.

## Reading the table

Run once on 2026-11-05 (git `d8d62b9`, script untracked); 96 registry lines = 24 configs x 2
symbols x 2 costs, 48 distinct runs. No post-hoc configs. Before the OOS run the script was smoke-run
on 2018-2019 (training data only, nothing logged) to check the targets; that pass did not change the grid.

**What wins is slow trend, and it is the bar the model families must clear.** The best baselines are
4h rules with ~30-day lookbacks: `ema_px_4h_180` (BTC 1.35 / ETH 1.37 Sharpe at 5 bps, 1.27 / 1.31 at
10 bps) and `tsmom_4h_180` (BTC 1.34 / ETH 1.47). Both beat buy-and-hold (0.84 / 0.89) at about half the
exposure, so `promotion` now requires Sharpe > 1.348 on BTC and > 1.467 on ETH from any candidate.
None of these baselines would pass promotion themselves: ~210 trades (< 500), positive months 41-49%
(< 60%), max drawdown -36% to -43% (limit -25%), worst year -11% to -23% (limit -10%). Their edge is
also concentrated: 2020-2021 returns were +76% to +428% a year for the top two, while H1 2026 is
-16% to +4% for every one of the top three and BTC 2025 was -8% to +6% (ETH 2025 was +57% to +80%). A model
that beats 1.35-1.47 over 2020-2026 mostly by riding 2020-2021 trend is not evidence of a new edge.
The 4h vs 1h comparison is consistent: `tsmom` and `ema_px` at 1h trade ~5x more than their 4h
30-day versions, score lower at base cost, and lose another 0.26-0.40 Sharpe going to 10 bps
(e.g. `tsmom_1h_168` BTC 0.62 -> 0.26).

**Vol-targeted buy-and-hold** (0.71-0.90) beats raw buy-and-hold slightly on Sharpe with the
0.2 rebalance band holding it to 18-171 fills in 6.5 years, but its drawdowns (-48% to -72%) are
still those of a mostly-long book. Its "trades = 1", "net bps/trade" and "PF = inf" columns are
artefacts: the harness ledger counts round trips from flat, and this book is never flat.

**Short-horizon mean reversion and time-of-day do not survive costs.** 15m mean reversion is negative
at base cost on both symbols (-0.86 to 0.00); 1h with k = 3 is +0.43 on both and drops to 0.20 / 0.23 at
10 bps (net 18-22 bps/trade at 5 bps, 8-12 at 10) on only 2% exposure and ~265 trades. The time-of-day rule is the worst family by far (-2.6 to -4.5 Sharpe at
5 bps): its hour choice is made on training data only, and its per-trade net is -6 to -8 bps at
10 bps round trip, i.e. a gross edge of only about +2 to +4 bps per trade that 3-5 round trips a day cannot pay
for. The lesson for the faster families: at 5 bps/side an intraday rule needs a gross edge well above
10 bps per round trip AND enough trades for the 500-trade and t-stat criteria; no short-horizon rule
here has both.

Caveats: rules 1-20 have no fitted state but the grid itself (lookbacks of 1 day to 30 days) was chosen
by me from common practice, not from the data; it is 24 configs per symbol and all 48 runs count in the
DSR N. The ranking among the trend rules is within noise of each other (t-stats of daily P&L 2.6-3.7),
so "best baseline" is the max of several correlated draws, which makes the baseline bar slightly
optimistic, i.e. harder for candidates, not easier.
